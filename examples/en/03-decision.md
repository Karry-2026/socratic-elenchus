# Example 3: A decision — "should I change track"

> Synthetic transcript; not about any real person. Demonstrates: facts before meaning → separating "feeling" from "fact" → substitution test → verdict-type closing.

**User**: I keep going back and forth on whether to change track. The one I am on feels pointless.

**Coach**: So the claim is that this track is not worth continuing. One fact first: is the "pointless" tied to something specific, or is it a general feeling?

**User**: There are specific things, but mostly it feels like there is no end point.

**Coach**: "No end point" — no promotion, or nothing accumulating?

**User**: Accumulating. Year one and year two look the same.

**Coach**: Give me one concrete action: what action would count as "accumulating"?

**User**: … the work settles into a method I can reuse next time.

**Coach**: On the current track, is there any action that could accumulate, that you simply are not doing?

**User**: Maybe — reviewing. But I do not have the time.

**Coach**: So is it that the track prevents accumulation, or that you have not left yourself the time? Those are different.

**User**: … the second one, mostly.

**Coach**: (Closing · this is my hypothesis) My hypothesis is that you do not want a change of track, you want the work to accumulate — is that right? The chain: you cannot describe the other track, but you can state the criterion for "accumulate"; and the current track may allow it, only your pace crowds it out. We skipped the contradiction step this round, because the question shifted from "which track" to "how time is allocated", which is not a clash of two positions. Weakest link: whether "no time" is a real shortage or a priority problem — untested. Open question: if you set aside a fixed review slot each week, would you still want to change track in three months?

---

## What this demonstrates

- **Facts first**: "something specific, or a general feeling?" — not straight into meaning.
- **Separating feeling from fact**: "pointless" split into "no promotion" and "not accumulating".
- **Substitution test**: what is the criterion for the other track — turning a vague "change" into a comparable condition.
- **No verdict for the user**: "the second one, mostly" is left for him to say.
- **Decision questions mapped onto the five steps**: definition = the criterion for accumulating; counterexample = "is there an action on the current track that accumulates"; revision = "the problem is time allocation, not the track".
