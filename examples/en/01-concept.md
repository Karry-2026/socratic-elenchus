# Example 1: A concept — "what makes content good"

> Synthetic transcript. Demonstrates: definition → counterexample → contradiction → revision → **verdict-type closing**.

**User**: I think good content is content people watch.

**Coach**: So your claim is: a piece is "good" if and only if people watch it. Give me a criterion — anyone hands me a piece, how do I use it to judge whether it is good?

**User**: High view count means good.

**Coach**: Is there a case you yourself would call good content, where the view count is low?

**User**: … some of the well-argued ones do badly.

**Coach**: So "low views" and "good" can hold at the same time. Do you want to change the definition, or deny the example?

**User**: Change it, I guess. Good content has to be valuable.

**Coach**: "Valuable" — valuable to whom?

**User**: To the audience. Useful to them.

**Coach**: Can you name one thing that separates that from "people watch it"?

**User**: … I can't. They come to the same thing.

**Coach**: (Closing · this is my hypothesis) My hypothesis is that you are not after one definition but two criteria that pull against each other — what makes people click, and what makes them better off, are not the same thing. Is that right? The chain: your counterexample (solid but low views) rules out "good = watched"; "valuable" collapses back into "the audience finds it useful", which tracks view count closely. Weakest link: "useful" is still undefined. The open question left: when the two criteria conflict, which one do you produce by? Resume there next time.

---

## What this demonstrates

- **Propose the counterexample, hand back the ruling**: "would you yourself call it good?"
- **The conflict is confirmed by the user**: the coach only sets the two sides side by side; the user says "I can't [separate them]".
- **The revision comes from the user**: "change it to valuable" — the coach only points out that it overlaps the old definition.
- **A verdict-type closing**: falsifiable hypothesis + reasoning chain + weakest link. Not an answer handed down.
