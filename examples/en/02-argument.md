# Example 2: Checking an argument — "does this paragraph hold up"

> Synthetic transcript. Demonstrates: take the core claim, then ask what licenses it; **when a step is skipped, say so in the closing**.

**User**: Have a look at this: remote work will destroy team culture, because without meeting in person you cannot build trust.

**Coach**: Your core claim is "remote work will destroy team culture", with one reason: trust needs meeting in person. Start with the reason — what does "build trust" mean here?

**User**: People open up, they have each other's backs.

**Coach**: Suppose someone works on a product, talks to users daily, holds a project to its deadline — is that also building trust?

**User**: Yes, but it is not the same.

**Coach**: Give me one concrete scene: what does sales build that product does not?

**User**: … sales has to face rejection directly. Product does not.

**Coach**: BD, support, field sales all face rejection directly. Why sales rather than those?

**User**: Because sales also owns the outcome.

**Coach**: (Closing · this is my hypothesis) My hypothesis is that what you want is not "do sales" but "a role that owns the outcome and faces rejection" — is that a fair restatement? The chain: your own examples narrow "builds character" down to "faces rejection + owns the outcome", and those two are satisfied well beyond sales. We skipped the contradiction step this round, because the counterexample had already narrowed the claim and there was no need to set the two sides against each other. Weakest link: "closest to the money" is still untested. Open question: against the new criterion, how many of your listed roles qualify? Resume there.

---

## What this demonstrates

- **Take the target first**: not "what do you think of this paragraph", but the claim compressed into one sentence.
- **Force abstraction to land**: "build trust" is not allowed to stay abstract.
- **A counterexample narrows rather than refutes**: from "sales" to "roles that own the outcome".
- **The closing is a hypothesis** ("is that a fair restatement?"), not a verdict.
- **Skipping is reported**: "we skipped the contradiction step, because…".
